Mesh

A square splits in four when cell size × gradient exceeds the threshold, and four siblings merge when all fall below a quarter of it. The O-layers are thin and wall-normal, about one and a half boundary-layer thicknesses when the thickness is left at 0, and the squares are cut to fit their outer edge. Columns of the O-layers split in two along the body where the streamwise jump is three times that threshold, with each new wall point placed on the true body outline, so a shock stays sharp down to the surface. Criterion, threshold, levels and interval apply immediately; the square limit, wake zone and layer thickness need a rebuild. Inside the wake zone, a band trailing downstream of the body, squares never grow beyond twice the size at the O-layer edge; set its length to 0 to switch it off. Steady runs adapt every ten times the interval.

Method

Checking whether the graphics processor can be used for computing…

Cell-centred finite volume on a body-fitted O-mesh, or on thin wall-normal O-layers inside a quadtree of squares that are cut to fit the layers' outer edge. On the adaptive mesh both parts refine as the flow develops: squares split in four, and O-layer columns split along the body. HLLC fluxes with second-order MUSCL reconstruction and a van Albada limiter; central viscous fluxes; Sutherland viscosity; adiabatic no-slip wall.

Turbulence uses the one-equation Spalart–Allmaras model, fully turbulent. Time accuracy comes from BDF2 with implicit inner iterations: block-tridiagonal solves along wall-normal lines and Gauss–Seidel sweeps around the body.

On the adaptive mesh the inner iterations use the true wave speeds where the flow is slow and smooth, which lets a low-Mach time step converge in about two thirds of the iterations it used to need.

The outer boundary applies one-dimensional Riemann invariants built from p∞, M∞, T∞ and the flow angle, so waves leave without reflecting. Below about Mach 0.1 the scheme turns slow and diffusive.
